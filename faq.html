Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Football Chaos

About the Program

What age is Football Chaos for?

Football Chaos is designed for kids ages 6-14. The activities scale beautifully across different skill levels. A 6-year-old enjoys the fun activities and learns basics. A 14-year-old digs into the tactical complexities and real coaching strategies. Parents tell us the program works great for mixed-age sibling groups too!

Do kids need to be on a soccer team to use Football Chaos?

Not at all! Football Chaos works perfectly for kids who play on teams AND kids who play casually in the backyard. Whether your child is competitive or just discovering the sport, this program fits. The 4-week training plan adapts to any skill level—beginners build fundamentals, and more advanced players work on game-changing tactics.

How much time does Football Chaos take per week?

Plan for approximately 2 hours per week total. That breaks down to:
• 15-20 minutes: Workbook activities and games
• 30-40 minutes: Drills from the training plan
• Optional extra: YouTube coaching videos (15-20 min)

It's designed to fit into a busy family schedule—not overwhelm it.

What equipment does my child need?

Just a soccer ball! Any size works—no specific brand or price point matters. The program adapts to:
• Backyard space (cones are optional)
• Park with open area
• Indoor space with smaller ball
• Full-size field

We've designed drills to work in almost any environment.

The Workbook

What's inside the Football Chaos workbook?

Parent Support

Is there a parent guide?

Yes! The Football Chaos package includes a dedicated 4-page Parent Quick Guide. It includes:
• Week-by-week checklists and what to expect
• 5 common challenges and how to handle them
• Safety tips and video guidance
• How to celebrate achievement

The guide helps parents support their child without needing to be a soccer coach.

My child isn't interested in soccer. Will this help?

Football Chaos is designed to BUILD interest. Many parents report that their child went from "I don't really like soccer" to practicing every day. The combination of learning from pro players, seeing real improvement, and the achievement structure can genuinely spark passion. That said, if your child has zero interest in the sport, this works best as a choice they make, not something forced on them.

What if my child gets frustrated or wants to quit?

The Parent Quick Guide has a whole section on this! Common triggers are "I'm not good enough" or "This is too hard"—these are growth mindset moments, and we give you exact strategies to handle them. Most kids who hit frustration points actually break through and end up MORE committed. It's in the guide.
